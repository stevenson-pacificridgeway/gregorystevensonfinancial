What Is an Indexed Annuity?

Growth linked to the market, protection from its losses — here's how an indexed annuity actually works, in plain English.

The simple version

An indexed annuity is a contract with an insurance company. You put in a lump sum (or a series of payments), and your money earns interest based on the performance of a market index — most often the S&P 500. The key difference from investing directly in the market: when the index goes up, you earn a share of that gain; when it goes down, your principal is protected and your credited interest is simply zero that year. You don't lose value from a market drop.

How the growth is calculated

You don't get 100% of the index's return. Insurers use a few levers to balance the downside protection they give you:

  • Cap rate — the maximum interest you can earn in a period (e.g., a 9% cap means that's the most you'll be credited even if the index returns 20%).
  • Participation rate — the percentage of the index's gain you receive (e.g., 70% participation on a 10% gain credits 7%).
  • Floor — usually 0%, meaning your worst year credits nothing rather than a loss.

What you give up

Protection and guarantees come with trade-offs. Your upside is limited by the cap or participation rate, you typically don't receive index dividends, and your money is meant to stay put for a set surrender period — taking out more than the allowed free amount early can trigger surrender charges. Indexed annuities reward patience, not day-trading.

Who an indexed annuity is for

They tend to fit people near or in retirement who want a portion of their savings to grow steadily without the risk of a market crash wiping out years of progress — and who value predictable, protected income over chasing the highest possible return. They are not a fit for money you may need immediately or for someone comfortable with full market risk.

The bottom line

An indexed annuity is a tool for protected growth and dependable retirement income. Whether it belongs in your plan depends on your timeline, goals, and the rest of your portfolio. The best next step is a no-pressure conversation.
